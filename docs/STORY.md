# LEVEL 256 — Story Bible

Single source of truth for the story. Every document, radio line, chapter card and ending must agree
with this file. A detail that is not written here does not exist yet: add it here first.

Everything is original: people, places, companies and events are invented. No real product or
trademark is named anywhere in the game.

## Logline

Halvard, a lake-country city in the north, the night of 13 to 14 January 1998. Ada Lind, 31, works
the night shift at **Depot 9**, the lost property office in the basement of Halvard Central Station.
At **02:56** the parcel chute drops a parcel nobody posted. Inside: a child's red mitten, left hand,
and a claim tag from a typewriter that has not been used since 1964:

> CLAIM 256 — one mitten, red, child's, left hand. Found: Lake Ostra, 14 January 1979.
> HOLD FOR: ADA LIND. — A.

Nineteen years ago tonight Ada's little sister Wren, seven years old, vanished on the frozen lake
behind their grandmother's house. A red mitten was the only thing ever found. This is the other one.

The freight elevator at the end of the archive has a button nobody pressed in thirty-four years. Someone
scratched a number above it: **256**.

## What the story is about

What families choose to forget, and what forgetting costs the people who are forgotten. Every place
Ada walks through is a place where someone was lost and someone else buried the truth of how. The
places did not disappear. They sank. Ada has to look at each buried truth before the place lets her
go, and each one brings her closer to the forty minutes of her own life she cannot remember.

## Tone rules

- Dread over shock. Quiet, then something is simply there. No sudden loud stings for their own sake.
- No gore. Death is a grab, a pull, darkness, cold. The horror is in what you understand.
- Documents are short: a reader finishes one in under 30 seconds. They are written by people, in their
  own voices, never as exposition.
- Otto is dry and kind and funny. The jokes make the dark darker.
- Rules are learned one at a time, each when it matters, never in one speech.
- Nobody in the game says "monster". People describe what they saw.

## The rules of the Underneath (in-world logic)

1. **What is lost and then buried sinks.** When something or someone is lost and the people who lost
   them bury the truth of how (a lie, a silence, a story told often enough), the lost thing sinks out
   of the world into the **Underneath**, and the place where it was lost sinks with it, exactly as it
   was at that moment: the fog, the snow, the hour.
2. **Buried truths grow teeth.** Each sunken place breeds its own inhabitants out of the truth buried
   there. They are not the dead. They are what the living refused to look at, given a body. That is why
   every place has its own kind, and why they behave like the lie that made them (the ferry's kind hide
   under water like a captain hid under a story; the train's kind check tickets).
3. **A place lets go when its loss is claimed.** Claiming means bringing the lost thing back to where it
   belongs in that place, which makes the truth known again. The way onward opens then, and only then.
   Otto calls the sunken places **shelves**: "You can't skip a shelf."
4. **Lost lights.** Things people stopped looking for glow faintly in the Underneath. The Eater, the
   oldest thing down there, eats them. Holding one makes the Eater afraid of you, for a few seconds.
5. **Time is filed, not kept.** A shelf is always the night it sank. Otto has been down for thirty-four
   years and believes it has been about one.
6. **Depot 9 is the door.** For ninety years everything lost in Halvard has passed through Depot 9.
   Lost things gather; under the place where they gather, the Underneath is thin. The freight elevator
   goes there if you know which button.
7. **The Clerk.** At the bottom of the Underneath is the **Index**, kept by the Night Clerk, who
   sends memos to Otto by pneumatic tube, signed "A." Otto has never seen her. Her handwriting is old and
   shaky. (She is Ada: see "The secret of the Clerk".)

## Cast

- **Ada Lind** (b. 1966) — the player. 31. Night clerk at Depot 9 since 1990. Careful, dry, tired,
  kind to strangers and hard on herself. Raised by her grandmother from 1983. She took the job at the
  lost property office without ever admitting to herself why. She has a forty-minute hole in her memory
  of 14 January 1979. She wears her old green scarf, the one she wore that day.
- **Wren Lind** (1971–1979) — Ada's sister. Seven. Small and loud, "like the bird", their grandmother
  said. Red snowsuit, red mittens. Drew birds on everything, and drew Ada from behind, walking away,
  because that was how she usually saw her. Never seen clearly in the game: a red figure at the end of a
  hallway, always walking ahead, always toward the next door.
- **Ingrid Lind** (1919–1996) — their grandmother. Lived in the house on the shore of Lake Ostra.
  Born in the village of Gammel Ostra, which was flooded in 1964 to make the reservoir: the lake is a
  drowned valley. Strong, quiet, never asked Ada what happened on the ice. Her last cassette tape,
  recorded a month before she died, is in Ada's locker. Ada has never played it.
- **Liv Lind** (b. 1946) — their mother. Left in 1983, lives in another country. Mentioned only.
- **Otto Brandt** (b. 1911) — night clerk at Depot 9 from 1946 to February 1964, when he took the
  freight elevator to the scratched button and did not come back. Talks to Ada on channel 9 of a
  walkie-talkie she finds in the Underneath. Catalogues everything ("Item 41,207: one umbrella, black,
  left with regrets."). Kind, precise, lonely, funny without meaning to be. He has forgotten his own
  first name; he signs "O.B." His brass Depot 9 badge, engraved OTTO BRANDT, is still in his desk.
- **Benny Sundqvist** — the day clerk. Leaves Ada handover notes. Only in documents.
- **Tor Holm** (1921–1977) — Ingrid's brother, Ada's great-uncle, engineer of the Ostra Dam.
- **Signe Holm** (1893–1964) — Ingrid's and Tor's mother. Refused to leave Gammel Ostra.

## Chapter by chapter

### Prologue — Depot 9 ("Night Shift")
Rain on the station's glass roof. Ada closes the counter. 02:56: the chute delivers the parcel.
The power fails; she resets the breaker in the sorting room. The 1979 ledger in the archive has an
entry in a hand she half recognises: "Claim 256 … Claimant: her sister, when she remembers." Otto's
office has been locked since 1964; the key is taped inside the ledger box. Inside: his coffee cup, his
badge, his notes on "the 256th level", the freight elevator key. Something tall moves at the far end of
the archive, sorting boxes in the dark, and is gone when the light reaches it. A small red figure
stands by the elevator for one second. Ada rides the elevator down.

### Chapter 1 — Level 256 ("Unclaimed")
Yellow rooms, wet carpet, the hum. Lost things everywhere: single gloves, umbrellas, a suitcase
with a child's name. A walkie-talkie on channel 9: Otto. He explains, one at a time, that this is the
sorting floor of everything lost; that the round thing that eats the lost lights is the Eater and it
is very old; that the flat men in the wallpaper only come off when your back is turned; that where
the hum gets loud, something is listening. The **Index Door** opens for four lost lights. When Ada
takes the fourth, the Eater wakes fully. Run.

### Chapter 2 — Saint Brigid ("Fog Bell")
The ferry as it sank: fog, listing decks, the car deck half flooded, the lounge full of passengers in
life jackets who do not move until a light touches them. Pim Rask's mother wrote to the inquiry that
her son was a good boy. Captain Aal's testimony says otherwise. The bridge logbook has a page torn
out; Ada finds it in the captain's cabin, in his whisky drawer. To leave she must lower lifeboat 2:
the davit key is on the bridge, the winch crank in the flooded engine room. Taking the crank wakes
everything under the water. When she puts the page back and the boat touches the water, somewhere
in the fog a bell rings by itself, steadily, the way a boy rang it until the end.

### Chapter 3 — Pinewood ("Double Feature")
A drive-in cinema in a pine forest north of Nordvik, Friday 22 August 1975, the last night of the
season. The film on the screen has no sound and never ends. Cars in rows, speaker posts, a snack bar,
a projection booth, the toilets at the edge of the trees, and the forest. The flyer promised a **lucky
stub draw**: bring your ticket stub to the booth window at the intermission and drop it in the can;
the projectionist, Lyle Hardy, draws the winner at the end of the second feature. Eight-year-old
Mikkel Strand (stub No. 1147) came to the window at 10:50; Lyle, a bottle open, told him to come back
after the reel change. At 11:05 Lyle, out behind the snack bar for air, saw the boy's torch go into the
trees behind the toilets after a **red light** deeper in. He did nothing, and told the police children
never came to the booth and he had seen nothing, because his licence and the booth were all he had.
He never drew the winner; he wrote to the mother nine times and never posted it. The search lost the
trail at the old hunting stand; a red boot was found north of it.
Mikkel's father turned the family wagon out of row five to point its headlights at the trees and left
them on all night, with a note under the wiper ("Stay where you can see the car lights"); the battery
died at four and he dropped his keys in the search (handed in to the kiosk's lost property book).
Ada finds the stub in the toilets and drops it in the can on the rewind bench: every speaker on the
field crackles and reads out number 1147. To leave she gets the wagon running: Lyle's booth battery,
petrol from the generator's drain tap (loud), the keys from the kiosk. The engine dies the first time
and everything hears it. The second time it catches, the chained gate has opened, and in the mirror
the screen goes white, then dark. The red lights are the Ushers'.

### Chapter 4 — Hollow Creek ("Seven Below")
A copper mine on the night of Saturday 3 March 1956, snow on the headframe. At 5:40 that morning the
roof came down in the east section of the 400-foot level; burning timber filled the east drift with
smoke. Seven men were beyond the fall: E. Nygaard, J. Vik, the brothers Tore and Karl Holmberg, A. Skog,
P. Moen, B. Ulstein. At 6:02 foreman **Arvid Lund** heard them knocking behind the fire door, seven and
seven and seven, counting off. He wedged the door against the smoke, then took their seven brass tags off
the station's **tally board** (a tag on the board means a man below), so the board showed the section
clear and the rescue went to the north fall instead. He went back once to listen, dropped the tin in the
dirt by the door, ran, and picked up only four tags. The station man's telephone book records all of it
("06.40 Still knocking"). Lund told the families they died at once, and kept the four tags in a tobacco
tin for twenty years; Ruth Holmberg asked him for her sons' tags and never got them.
In the sunken mine Ada needs a **canary** to go down (the foreman's own rule), rides the cage to the
station, finds the tin in the shift boss's cabin and the other three tags in the dirt by the fire door
where the knocking still comes from, and hangs all seven back on their hooks: the knocking stops, and
Wren's fifth drawing slides out under the door. The cage motor needs the emergency generator (diesel
from the fuel store at the bottom of the gas-filled south drift; firedamp lies under the roof, so you
keep low). The cage stops once halfway up the shaft and must be restarted by hand while something comes
down the wall toward its roof.

### Chapter 5 — Weisshorn ("Whiteout")
Berghotel Weisshorn, 2 914 m, at the top station of a cable car, Monday 28 February 1983, in a
blizzard: fireplace hall, bar, reception, Greta Imhof's office, dining room, kitchen and cold room, the
guest corridor, the ski room, and across the terrace and sixty metres of blowing snow the cable car
station. The telephone line was down from seven. At 8:15 the post sledge brought the avalanche service's
telegram: danger 5, close the upper runs and the upper station. Greta Imhof, 52, signed for it, put it
in her office stove, and wrote nothing about it in her weather book; it was the hotel's first full week
since 1979 ("We need this week" — her note to the cook, Anton). At 9:30 the ski school went up: the
Aebi family (Peter, Ursula and Lisa, 10), J. Coulter, Ruth Fankhauser, and the instructor Leo Brunner,
29. The upper slopes released at 2:40 p.m. Mrs Coulter, who stayed in, left on 2 March. At the inquiry
Greta said no warning had reached the hotel. The telegram never burned. Ada lifts it out of the ash and
pins it back on the reception board; the wind drops for a breath, the station's lights come on, and with
the cable car's master key (on its hook by the cold room) she starts the car and boards as something
comes across the snow after the sound of the motor. The cold outside is its own danger; the Frozen move
only while she is warm.

### Chapter 6 — Gammel Ostra ("Low Water")
The drowned village under the reservoir, drained: mud streets, a church, houses with the waterline
across the wallpaper, boats on rooftops. Ada's great-grandmother Signe Holm refused to leave. Her son
Tor, the dam's engineer, told her the gates would close on the ninth of October. They closed on the
second. Ingrid never spoke of it. The Lind women forget. Signe's music box goes back on her mantel;
then the water starts coming back, and the only way out is up the dam's service ladder.

### Chapter 7 — Nordlys Express ("Last Stop")
A night sleeper train that never arrives: platform, sleeping cars, dining car, baggage car, locomotive.
On 19 December 1990 conductor Edvin Saether put fifteen-year-old Lina Berg off at Kvitfjell halt, in the
snow, at night, because she had no ticket. She had bought one; it was under her seat. He said nothing
about the girl at the inquiry into the "missing passenger". The Conductor checks tickets. Ada finds a
ticket in the dining car; then she finds Lina's, and the Conductor stops checking and starts hunting.
The way out is the emergency brake in the locomotive, before the train passes Kvitfjell again.

### Chapter 8 — Falk's Carnival ("Bright Lights")
A travelling fair on Halvard harbour after closing: carousel, mask stall, funhouse, ferris wheel,
ghost train. In 1984 the ghost train burned. Owner's son Kasper Falk, nineteen, had been smoking in it;
the old clown Hugo Brecht, "Pipo", was blamed, fired, and walked off into the October night with his
suitcase. Pipo's red nose goes back on his trailer mirror; the ghost train starts by itself, and it is
the only way through the fence.

### Chapter 9 — Lake Ostra ("The Ice")
14 January 1979, 15:40, a snowstorm coming. Grandmother's house on the shore, warm, with the radio on.
The frozen lake, the ice-fishing huts where the older kids were, the pier, the boathouse, and far out,
the drowned church's spire standing out of the ice. Wren is out there somewhere.

**What happened.** Ada, twelve, was going to the huts to meet the older kids. Wren followed her onto
the ice in her red snowsuit. Ada told her to go home; Wren would not; Ada pushed her hand away and said
*get lost*. Wren turned back toward the shore and, in the whiteout, walked the wrong way, toward the
dark ice over the old river channel where the current keeps it thin. At the huts, Ada heard the ice
crack and decided it was nothing, because the older kids were watching her. Forty minutes later she
walked home and told her grandmother that Wren had never come out on the ice, that she thought Wren
was at home. The search began in the house and the woods. The mitten was found on the ice two days
later. Ada has not remembered any of it since.

**The Hush** wears Ada's green scarf. It is what she buried, and it does not want to be dug up.

## Endings

- **Thaw** (true ending). At the thin ice, Ada says it out loud: what she said, where she sent Wren,
  what she told their grandmother. Wren turns around. Ada gives her the left mitten. Wren walks home,
  toward the lights of the house, and does not look back, and that is all right. Ada wakes at the
  Depot 9 counter at 06:10 with the parcel open in front of her. She files Claim 256 as CLAIMED, phones
  the Halvard police about a nineteen-year-old case, and plays her grandmother's tape. In April, when
  the ice goes, divers search the old river channel. Wren is buried beside Ingrid.
- **Snowfall** (stay). Ada says Wren just vanished. The Hush takes off the scarf; under it is Ada's own
  face at twelve. The snow covers the lake. A memo goes up the tube from the Index in a new hand:
  "Night Clerk, Level 256. First shift." Otto: "Welcome to the night shift."
- **Morning** (secret: Thaw, plus all eight of Wren's drawings found and Otto's badge returned to him at
  the Index Door). As Thaw, and at 06:10 an old man in a 1964 coat walks out of the Depot 9 freight
  elevator, says "Brandt. Otto. I remember now", asks what year it is, and laughs.

## The secret of the Clerk

The Night Clerk who signs "A." is Ada: an old Ada, from a turning of this same night in which she chose
Snowfall and stayed, and kept the Index for a long time, and finally sent the mitten up the chute to the
girl she had been, to give her the chance again. Clues: the memos' handwriting (Ada's, shaking); the
Clerk knows things only Ada could know; the tag says "Hold for: Ada Lind" rather than "Claimant".
In Thaw and Morning, the final card shows a second tag in the Depot 9 tray in the same old hand:
"Claimed. At last. — A." Otto never learns who she was.

## Wren's drawings (eight, collectible)

1. In the parcel: a bird on a fence, and a tall girl with a green scarf walking away. "ADA".
2. The Underneath: a yellow room, and a round thing with a mouth, and a bird flying over it.
3. Saint Brigid: a boat in fog, a boy with a bell, the bird on the mast.
4. Pinewood: a big screen in the trees and a little boy holding a torch, looking back.
5. Hollow Creek: seven little lamps in a line under the ground.
6. Gammel Ostra: a church under water with fish, and a lady at a window.
7. Nordlys Express: a train with every window lit, and one girl standing in the snow.
8. Falk's Carnival: a clown waving goodbye with a suitcase.

Every drawing has the bird. In every drawing that has Ada, Ada has her back turned.
